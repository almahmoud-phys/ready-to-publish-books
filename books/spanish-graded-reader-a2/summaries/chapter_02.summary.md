# Story 02 — El pan de las cinco

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Follow a conversation driven by questions.
- **Grammar introduced:** Direct-question `cuándo` (a recorded house decision) and `por qué`; Story 01 grammar remains available.
- **Vocabulary progression:** 13 new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 29.

## Story state
Ana goes to Rosa Delgado’s bakery when it opens at five, carrying Tomás Ferrer’s letter. Rosa habitually answers Ana’s questions with questions. When Ana exposes the name, Rosa does not ask who Tomás is or what the letter says; she asks where the letter was. Ana lies that it was found in the street near the pier rather than at the kiosk. Rosa gives Ana far more bread than she ordered and refuses payment. Ana recognizes the bread as Rosa’s non-answer: people in Puerto Lento avoid forbidden questions by giving bread, saying “No hay nada,” or looking at the sea. Ana does not eat the bread.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions test Rosa’s question-led replies, her question about the letter’s location, and Ana’s response to the bread.
- “Seis cosas” gives six out-of-order events for the reader to reorder into the story’s sequence.

## Canon carried forward
- Rosa runs the bakery, opens at five, and knows Tomás’s name.
- Rosa’s concern is the letter’s location, not Tomás’s identity.
- Ana’s first deliberate concealment is the kiosk as the letter’s origin.
- **New canon candidates:** None.
