# Story 10 — La luz, otra vez

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Read a full mixed-tense story and explain why characters act.
- **Grammar introduced:** None; this is the capstone.
- **Vocabulary progression:** Four new normalized types are closed; volume total is 100.

## Story state
Almost a year after Story 01, Beto has returned in another boat and plans to leave again on the next one. During the intervening months, Ana has stored Lucía’s monthly letters in a box under her bed and leaves only the newest letter on the counter. On the next anniversary of the annual ritual, Ana sees Lucía’s lamp, takes a boat alone for the first time, and reaches Doña Lucía. Lucía confirms that each month she places a letter on the kiosk counter and, once a year, rows out with a lamp to place another letter in the water. She knows Tomás is not in the sea; she writes because she has words for him and because the sea does not answer with “no.” Ana rows back and publicly tells Beto and Rosa the letter and light truth. Rosa responds first: she says she read the list but did not see Tomás on the boat, then says it is good to speak. Only afterward does Beto take up and put down his bag, look at Rosa and Ana, and decide not to leave because Ana’s truth means the town now speaks. The next morning Beto remains, Rosa opens the bakery, Ana opens the kiosk, and the letter stays on the counter. The phrase “no hay nada” is absent.

## Exercise proof
- Eight distinct comprehension questions include four `por qué` questions about Ana’s solo boat, Lucía’s water letter, Beto’s choice, and Rosa’s response.
- The Spanish capstone has two prompts: readers answer why Ana goes to the light and why Beto stays, using one or two sentences in Spanish for each answer.

## Canon carried forward
- The light is Lucía’s annual lamp and her sea letter is symbolic, not a delivery route; Ana stores the monthly kiosk letters and keeps only the newest one on the counter.
- Beto stays after Ana speaks publicly; the town’s silence is broken.
- Tomás’s actual location remains unresolved for a later volume and must not be falsely promised as resolved.
- Stories 01–09 span roughly five weeks; Story 10 explicitly returns almost a year after Story 01 for the next annual sea-letter ritual.
- **New canon candidates:** None.
