# Story 06 — No me gusta esperar

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Read opinions and reactions.
- **Grammar introduced:** `gustar`-type verbs and the indirect-object system.
- **Vocabulary progression:** Six new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 68.

## Story state
After Beto’s departure, Ana waits near the pier and publicly asks who leaves the letters at her kiosk. The public question costs her: Miguel, the old fisherman, and other residents object to disturbing the town’s quiet. Rosa finally says the letters arrive monthly, have done so for years, and are left by “esa señora.” She explains that each prior kiosk worker knew to leave the letters alone; Ana alone was not told. Ana stops asking in public. That evening she moves the letter from beneath her bed to the kiosk counter, the place where the woman leaves it, and changes her chair to face the letter. At closing she leaves it on the counter.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions check Ana’s impatience, the people at the pier, the town’s objection, Rosa’s explanation, and the counter decision.
- A six-item attribution exercise asks who expresses each opinion or reaction, including the old fisherman, a woman, Miguel, Ana, and the town’s wish for quiet.

## Canon carried forward
- Doña Lucía has left a letter at the kiosk monthly for years.
- Previous kiosk workers knew the arrangement; Ana was deliberately excluded.
- Rosa’s “esa señora” gesture seeds Lucía’s identity without naming her there.
- Ana returns the letter to the kiosk’s counter and stops public questioning.
- **New canon candidates:** None.
