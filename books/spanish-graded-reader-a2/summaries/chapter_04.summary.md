# Story 04 — Todos los días lo mismo

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Track the town’s daily routine and recognize a break in it.
- **Grammar introduced:** Reflexive routine verbs, time expressions, frequency adverbs, and habitual/cyclic present.
- **Vocabulary progression:** 14 new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 59.

## Story state
Ana breaks her own routine to wait before dawn and identify the person who leaves the letter. The town’s repeated timetable is established: Miguel goes to the port, Rosa opens the bakery, boats leave, Beto opens the boat shed, and Ana opens the kiosk at night. When Beto fails to open the shed, Ana finds Miguel there with a box of old papers and a roster bearing Tomás Ferrer’s name. Miguel explains that Tomás’s possessions, name, and bakery table remain because nobody will decide that he is gone. He repeats “No hay nada.” Ana understands the phrase is not a factual denial but the town’s refusal to make that decision.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions check the normal timetable, today’s disruption, the papers in the shed, and the meaning of “No hay nada.”
- “Hoy” asks readers to identify the three changes from the normal routine among six statements.

## Canon carried forward
- Miguel keeps or accesses Tomás’s old work papers at the boat shed.
- The town has never declared Tomás gone.
- “No hay nada” is a shared refusal, not merely Beto’s phrase.
- **New canon candidates:** None.

## Setup
The monthly supply boat gives Ana a possible way to remove the letter and exposes Beto’s plan to leave.
