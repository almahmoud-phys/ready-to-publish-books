# Story 09 — Nadie dijo nada

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Read past narration with licensed irregular preterites.
- **Grammar introduced:** Irregular preterite by specified lemma in first- and third-person singular.
- **Vocabulary progression:** Five new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 96.

## Story state
Ana brings the letter to Doña Lucía’s house, but Lucía says it belongs to Tomás and to the kiosk rather than to her. Lucía agrees with Rosa that Tomás’s name was on the boat list, then supplies the decisive distinction: Tomás was in the port that morning but did not board. He came home with a small bag, said he was leaving, traveled by road, and never returned. Lucía never corrected the town’s sea-loss story because a son lost at sea is mourned publicly while a son who walked away is not. She writes letters because Tomás had worked at the kiosk, not because she expects a reply. Ana keeps Lucía’s confidence as she kept Rosa’s, tells Rosa nothing at the bakery, and returns the letter to the kiosk counter.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions test Ana’s visit, the crew-list distinction, Tomás’s departure, Lucía’s silence, and Ana’s confidence.
- Six “Preguntas para pensar” require textual inference about the two accounts, Lucía’s sparse house, the lamp by the letter, the sea-versus-road contrast, Lucía’s solitude, and Ana’s final chair position.

## Canon carried forward
- Tomás did not board the lost boat; he left by road that morning and never returned.
- Rosa’s crew-list account and Lucía’s personal account are compatible rather than mutually exclusive.
- Lucía has deliberately left the town’s public story unchanged.
- Ana now holds both women’s confidences.
- **New canon candidates:** None.

## Setup
The annual light must reveal Lucía’s sea ritual and force Ana to choose whether to speak publicly.
