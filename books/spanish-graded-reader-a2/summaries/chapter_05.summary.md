# Story 05 — Mañana viene el barco

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Understand plans and predictions.
- **Grammar introduced:** `ir a` + infinitive, `querer`/`poder` + infinitive, and future-time markers.
- **Vocabulary progression:** Three new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 62.

## Story state
Beto explains that the supply boat arrives once a month, brings provisions, and is the only practical way to send something away or leave Puerto Lento. Ana plans to give the letter to its captain to end her involvement. That night Beto says he will leave on the boat because nobody in the town answers anything. At the dock, the captain asks who Tomás Ferrer is. Seeing Beto leave because of the same culture of evasion, Ana closes her hand around the letter rather than give a stranger another non-answer. Beto boards and the boat departs; Ana returns the letter to its established place under her bed.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions test the boat’s schedule and supplies, Ana’s plan, Beto’s departure, the captain’s question, and Ana’s decision to keep the letter.
- The eight-item “ahora” versus future exercise separates present statements from plans or events that are going to happen.

## Canon carried forward
- The supply boat comes monthly and connects the town to elsewhere.
- Beto leaves because the town refuses answers.
- Ana deliberately keeps the letter.
- **New canon candidates:** None.

## Setup
Ana’s unwillingness to wait another month leads her to ask publicly who leaves the letters.
