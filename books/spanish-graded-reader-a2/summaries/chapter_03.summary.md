# Story 03 — La mesa que nadie usa

## Contract and learning progression
- **Delivered reader capability:** Hold a physical description and relationships in mind without translating word by word.
- **Grammar introduced:** Adjective agreement, descriptive `ser`/`estar`, `tener` expressions, and basic reflexives.
- **Vocabulary progression:** 16 new normalized types are closed; cumulative total is 45.

## Story state
On a crowded Sunday, Ana wants the only empty bakery table but Rosa forbids her to use the window table, which is clean, set with coffee and bread, and avoided by everyone. An old fisherman sits there, orders “as always,” knows Ana’s name, and says she now has Lucía’s son’s old kiosk job. He identifies the table as the absent son’s and Rosa’s daily ritual. Ana therefore learns that her job was Tomás Ferrer’s and that Tomás is Lucía’s son. She sees an older woman outside silently watching the window table; the chapter identifies her in the text only as the woman, while the bible defines her as Doña Lucía.

## Exercise proof
- Five comprehension questions check the window-table prohibition, the old fisherman’s description, Ana’s known name, the table’s owner, and the woman outside.
- “Las personas” asks readers to match eight descriptions, actions, and quoted words to the correct characters.

## Canon carried forward
- The window table belongs to Tomás in the town’s ritual sense; Rosa keeps it set daily.
- Tomás is Lucía’s son; the old fisherman knew Tomás and knows Ana because the town knows who inherited the kiosk.
- Doña Lucía watches the bakery from outside.
- **New canon candidates:** None.
