## Direct-sales landing copy — T1 recommended candidate

# Read the story. Keep the thread.

You know some Spanish. You can recognize familiar words and perhaps fill in a conjugation table. But a full page still turns into a translation exercise: stop, decode, forget the thread, quit.

**The Letter at Puerto Lento** is for the adult learner who wants to read a real Spanish story for meaning.

In a small coastal town, Ana finds a letter addressed to someone nobody will name. Each of the ten linked stories gives her—and you—one more reason to continue: a bakery that opens before dawn, an unused table, a boat that may arrive tomorrow, old letters, two accounts of one year, and a light that finally has an ordinary explanation.

## What this book includes

- **Ten linked Spanish stories** with a continuous adult mystery rather than disconnected textbook vignettes.
- **A gradual reading path:** the book moves from short present-tense narratives toward a longer capstone that uses the time frames introduced along the way.
- **Brief Spanish comprehension prompts** after each story, designed to move from recalling events to explaining why characters act.
- **A declared Latin-American Spanish convention.** The project checks selected mutually exclusive locale markers; it does not claim to represent every Latin-American region.
- **Documented text controls.** The Spanish source is checked for cumulative lexical coverage, new-word load, selected locale-marker consistency, and grammar sequencing. These are reproducible checks, not CEFR certification.

This is not audio, a certification course, or a promise of fluency on a schedule. It is a structured way to finish a connected Spanish story—and then another one.

**Begin with the letter.**
