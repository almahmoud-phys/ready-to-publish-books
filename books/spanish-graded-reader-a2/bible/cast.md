# Cast — Volume 1

Five named living characters, plus one absent name (Tomás). A vocabulary-controlled reader cannot afford a large cast: every name is a
proper noun the reader must hold, and holding names competes with holding meaning. All names are
common across Latin America and easy to decode for an English speaker.

Names are passed to the checker via `--names` so they never count as out-of-vocabulary.

| Name | Age | Role | Speech register | First appears | Pays off |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **Ana Morales** | 34 | Runs the pier kiosk at night. Point-of-view character in every story. | Plain, short sentences. Observes before judging. Asks direct questions. | 01 | 10 — she is the only one who goes out to the light |
| **Beto Morales** | 29 | Ana's younger brother. Works the boat shed. Wants to leave. | Terse and evasive. Says "no hay nada" more than once — it becomes his tell. | 01 | 05 (plans to leave), 10 (stays) |
| **Rosa Delgado** | 58 | Runs the bakery, opens at five. Knows everything, says little. | Answers questions with questions. Never lies outright. | 02 | 08 — she finally tells the 1998 story |
| **Doña Lucía Ferrer** | 71 | Walks the beach at night. The town treats her as strange. | Speaks in short declaratives. No hedging. Says exactly what she saw. | 03 | 09 — her account contradicts Rosa's |
| **Miguel Ortiz** | 41 | Harbour mechanic. Minor but recurring — the town's ordinary voice. | Practical, technical, uninterested in the light. | 04 | 04 — found inside the shed, looking for something |
| **Tomás Ferrer** | — | The name on the letter. Doña Lucía's son. Never appears alive. | — (absent by definition) | 01 (as a name only) | 08–10 — the argument over whether he boarded |

## Rules that keep continuity honest

- **Nobody is a villain.** The 1998 story is a grief the town agreed not to discuss, not a crime.
  A mystery-with-a-culprit would push this toward genre plotting the vocabulary cannot support.
- **Ana never solves anything by being clever.** She solves it by going out to look, which is the
  thematic point and keeps the plot inside the declared vocabulary.
- **"No hay nada" is the town's phrase, not just Beto's.** Verbatim from Beto in 01 and from
  Miguel in 04. It must **NOT** appear in story 10 — its absence is the ending. A tell that belongs
  to one character is a quirk; a tell the whole town shares is the theme.
- **Ana is new to the kiosk — three months.** She inherited Tomás's job. This must be established
  in story 01; the entire letter mechanism rests on it (`bible/letters-causal-ledger.md`).
- **Doña Lucía is Tomás's mother.** Story 03 explicitly establishes that Tomás is Lucía's son;
  the woman watching the table there is still unnamed in the prose. Story 06's "esa señora" and
  Story 07's letter delivery then connect the known family fact to the recurring woman. The review
  was right that inference the contracts never plant is wishful thinking, not craft.
- **The stranger at the table in story 03 is not a ghost.** He is an old fisherman who knew Tomás,
  and he calls Ana by name because everyone in a town this size knows her. The story is written so
  a first-time reader may briefly think otherwise — that is the hook — but nothing supernatural is
  ever asserted, because the volume resolves physically in story 10 and a cheat here would break it.
- Ages and roles are fixed here. Any change is a continuity break, not a revision.
