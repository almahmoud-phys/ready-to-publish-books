## Supported shelf conventions

1. **Oversized category language is the reliable signal.** `SPANISH` and/or `SHORT STORIES` dominate all three and survive thumbnail reduction.
2. **The descriptive product hierarchy leads.** None relies on a fiction-style narrative title to establish the shelf.
3. **Contrast is consistent; palette is not.** White/orange, purple/yellow, and orange/multicolor all appear. There is no evidence for one mandatory category color.
4. **Secondary qualifiers are fragile.** Beginner level, adult positioning, author, publisher promise, and illustration detail disappear first.
5. **Typography carries category recognition.** The sample does not support one shared imagery convention.
6. **The visible shelf leaves an adult-literary gap.** One comp uses a child-adjacent person-and-dog illustration, one uses crowded learning icons, and one uses a travel/Barcelona scene. A restrained mystery can differentiate, but only if category typography remains explicit.

## Implications for this book

- *The Letter at Puerto Lento* cannot signal Spanish learning by itself. Every viable concept needs a prominent `A2 SPANISH GRADED READER` hierarchy or badge.
- The full candidate subtitle is too long to carry the 100px signal. It belongs in metadata/full-size copy; the cover should extract a short category line.
- “Adult” and “Latin American” should not depend on tiny text. Adult positioning must come from typography, restraint, and avoidance of cartoon/workbook cues; the Latin-American convention remains in metadata unless a compact, readable line survives.
- A detailed coastal scene is the highest-risk direction because its category signal can disappear. It must be tested against typography-first alternatives.

## Three distinct test hypotheses

1. **Category-first literary bridge:** warm cream field, enormous `SPANISH`, visible A2 badge, restrained envelope/postmark mark. Conform first, differentiate second.
2. **Typographic series system:** deep navy field, modular `A2 / SPANISH / GRADED READER` hierarchy, amber postal/horizon motif. Maximum repeatability and thumbnail force.
3. **Fiction-first coastal mystery:** harbor-at-night scene and narrative title, protected by a large high-contrast category band. Maximum adult-fiction differentiation, highest category-confusion risk.

These are hypotheses until rendered and inspected. The approval mockups are deterministic vector comps, not final AI-generated cover art and not KDP-ready assets.
