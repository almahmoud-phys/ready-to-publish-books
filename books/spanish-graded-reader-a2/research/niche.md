## Persona

**An adult English speaker, roughly 25–60, who has finished a beginner app or course** (Duolingo,
Babbel, a semester of night classes) and can recognise words and conjugation tables, but cannot
read continuous Spanish. They stop to translate every sentence, lose the thread, and quit. They
are not a student, not a hobbyist collector of grammar, and not a child. They buy on Kindle, often
read inside Kindle Unlimited, and want to feel like a reader rather than a pupil.

**Explicit non-reader:** children and classroom groups, absolute zero-knowledge beginners,
heritage speakers wanting literary Spanish, and anyone seeking exam preparation (DELE/SIELE).

## Gap statement

On this shelf the teaching method is not the problem — the prose is. The gap is a graded reader
**an adult is not embarrassed to be seen reading**, in **one declared Latin American locale**, with
**correctness that is mechanically proven rather than asserted**.

## Differentiation contract

1. **Stories written for adults.** No talking animals, no children's-primer register, no textbook
   framing; every story has a narrative reason to turn the page.
   *Cites Ramona Mead 2019-10-19, Mikael 2021-10-05, soph 2019-08-01.*
   *Verification: editorial only — judged at stage 3/4 against the rubric. The one promise no
   machine check can prove, and the highest risk in the book.*
2. **One declared Latin American locale**, on the cover and enforced throughout.
   *Cites Shawn 2019-08-05.* *Verification: mechanical — `graded_reader_check.py`
   `locale_violations()` fails the build on a single `vosotros`.*
3. **Every Spanish sentence passes a published, reproducible check** before it ships.
   *Cites soph 2019-08-01.* *Verification: partly mechanical — coverage is built and green;
   morphology, alignment and back-translation are NOT yet built.*

## Asset feasibility

Text-only interior. No photography, no screenshots, no hardware, no field testing. Verification is
fully computational. Cover is AI-images per ADR-005.

Flagged risks: several comps ship an **audio download** which we do not have and must never imply;
Pandoc metadata hardcodes `lang: en`, so Spanish spans need their own language tagging before
export or screen readers mispronounce the whole book; and the check suite is real engineering that
must exist before drafting, not after.

**Vocabulary baseline:** hermitdave/FrequencyWords `es_50k.txt` (OpenSubtitles-derived, **MIT**,
commercially usable), top 2,000 forms — see `assets/WORDLIST-PROVENANCE.md`. A frequency proxy,
**not a CEFR A1 certification**, and the charter forbids claiming otherwise. MIT attribution
belongs in the back matter.

## What was NOT collected, and why the verdict does not depend on it

- **Amazon 1–3 star reviews** — sign-in wall; ADR-008 bans evasion. Goodreads substituted, with the
  weaker-evidence label attached. The differentiation contract is therefore moderate-confidence.
- **Review velocity / latest review dates** — same wall.
- **Head-term result count** — Amazon caps the display; the sub-niche counts carry enterability.

None of these changes the two facts the GO rests on: two verified sub-50k comps in an evergreen,
indie-winnable category, and a rising five-year trend.
