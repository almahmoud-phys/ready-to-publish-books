# English Translations

Use this section as a check, not as a first read.

Read the Spanish story all the way through first, even if some sentences stay
unclear. Guessing from context is how reading builds. Then come here and see
what you got right. Reading the English first turns the story into a
translation exercise, and the Spanish stops doing any work.

These translations follow the Spanish sentence by sentence, in the same order,
so you can find any line quickly. That makes them a little plainer than natural
English in places. Where the Spanish repeats a word, the English repeats it too.

Two things the English cannot show you:

**Usted and tú.** Spanish marks who you are talking to. Ana says *usted* to
Doña Lucía all the way to the end, even in the boat, even after everything is
said. English has only "you", so that distance disappears in translation. Watch
it in the Spanish — it carries much of what these people feel about each other.

**Doña.** *Doña Lucía* stays *Doña Lucía*. It is a title of respect for an
older woman, common across Latin America, with no English equivalent that is
not either too formal or faintly mocking.

The comprehension questions and exercises are not translated. They are at the
back of the book, in Spanish, on purpose — answering a Spanish question in
Spanish is the point.
