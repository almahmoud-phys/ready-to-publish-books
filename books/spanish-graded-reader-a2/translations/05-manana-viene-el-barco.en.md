# Story 5 — Tomorrow the Boat Comes

At six in the morning, Beto opens the boat shed. Always at six. Ana is at the
door. She has the letter.

"Tomorrow the boat comes," says Beto.

On the door there is a paper with the day and time of the boat. Ana reads it twice. The day is clear.

Ana touches the letter. It has the name of Tomás Ferrer.

"I want to give this letter to the captain," says Ana. "Then I won't have it anymore."

Beto looks at the letter, but he does not touch it.

"The captain doesn't know everyone in town," he says.

"He might know the name."

"He might," says Beto.

Ana looks at the sea. The boat comes once a month. It brings bread, coffee and water. Then it leaves
again. Next week it does not come. Next month it arrives again.

The boat is the only way to send things out of Puerto Lento. It is also the only way to leave
town. If Ana does not give the letter tomorrow, she waits a month.

Ana does not want to wait a month. She wants to give the letter away. She wants to have a quiet
night, like always.

"Can I come with you tomorrow?" she asks.

"Yes. The boat arrives at seven."

"And can the captain take the letter?"

"He can take a letter," says Beto. "But he doesn't know the name."

Ana says nothing. She has the letter in her hand.

In the afternoon, Ana works in the kiosk. At seven she opens the window. The sea is calm. On
the counter there is coffee, bread, water and a cup. Everything is in its place, but Ana is not calm.

At twelve, Beto arrives. He drinks coffee and looks at the street.

"Beto, are you leaving on the boat?" Ana asks.

"Yes. I'm going tomorrow."

Ana puts the cup down on the table.

"Why?"

Beto looks at the sea. Then he looks at Ana.

"Because in this town nobody answers anything."

Ana thinks about Rosa. She thinks about Miguel. She thinks about the table by the window and about all the names
that are in papers and boxes. Nobody answers. Everyone looks at the sea.

"Where are you going?" she asks.

"I don't know. I'm going to leave."

"And when are you coming back?"

Beto drinks the coffee. He says nothing.

Ana waits a moment. Then she puts the letter next to the cup.

"Tomorrow I'll give it away," she says.

"Yes," says Beto. "Tomorrow."

At one, Ana finishes her work. Beto leaves first. Ana goes out behind him. Ana closes the kiosk
door.

"At seven," she says.

Ana takes the letter. She checks the time many times.

In the morning, the pier is crowded. There are people and boxes. The boat comes in slowly. It is big. The
captain comes down and looks at the people.

Beto is carrying a small bag with his things. He goes up to the boat.

"Are you going to give the letter?" he asks.

Ana holds out the letter. The captain looks at it.

"Who is it for?" he asks.

"Tomás Ferrer."

The captain looks at the name. Then he looks at Ana.

"Who is Tomás Ferrer?"

Ana has the letter in one hand. The captain waits. Beto waits too.

Ana looks at her brother. Beto has one foot on the boat. He is leaving because nobody answers.

Ana closes her hand around the letter. The captain looks at Ana and boards the boat.

Beto boards behind him. The boat leaves the pier. Ana has the letter in her hand.

Then she goes back to her work. She works until one. Then she closes the kiosk and goes home. She puts the
letter under the bed. That is its place. Then she closes the door.
