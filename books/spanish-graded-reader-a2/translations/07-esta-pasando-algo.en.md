# Story 7 — Something Is Happening

Tonight Ana wants to wait inside the kiosk all night. She wants to see who comes through the
door and puts a letter on the counter.

At seven she opens the kiosk. There is coffee, bread and water. There is a letter on the counter. The name of
Tomás Ferrer is facing up. Ana does not touch the letter.

Her chair faces the letter. Ana moves the chair. Now the chair faces the door. The window is
to one side. Through the window she can see the pier, the beach and the sea.

Ana knows the lady comes at night. Rosa says she comes once a month. Tonight there is a letter.
Ana is not going to sleep. She is not going home. She does not want to wait another month.

At night, the pier is almost empty. Ana is in the chair and she is watching the door. The door
is closed. The street is quiet.

Then, through the window, Ana sees a light on the water. It is white and it makes noise. It is a boat
passing far away.

The boat's light is moving. Then it is still. Then it changes again. Ana wants
to watch the light. She also wants to watch the door. She cannot watch both things at once.

Ana moves the chair a little. Now she can see the light through the window and the door in front of her. But
the light is far away and the door is close. Ana leaves the light and watches the door.

The letter is in front of her. Ana listens to the water. A rope knocks against a post. Then the engine
of a boat sounds far away and disappears. The window lets in cold air. A piece of paper moves next to
the letter. Ana leaves it there.

While Ana is watching the door, the light is moving on the water. The sea is making
noise. Ana listens to the clock on the wall. Every minute seems long. Ana drinks coffee. The coffee is cold.
She rubs her hands. She looks at the letter. She does not touch it.

At three, the wind makes noise outside. Ana stands up. The street is empty. She goes back to her chair.
The boat's light changes place on the water. Ana sees it for a moment and then she watches the door.

While Ana watches the door, the boat passes far away. The engine makes noise for a moment. Then the noise
leaves the harbour. Ana listens to the water again. The letter is still on the counter. The door is still closed.
Ana looks at the letter, but she does not touch it. She watches the door. She wants to know who comes. She does not want to watch the boat.

At four, the door opens.

A woman comes into the kiosk. She is old. In one hand she has a white letter.

Ana knows the woman. She is the woman who looks at the table by the window from the street. She is the lady who
comes to the beach at night. Ana remembers a phrase: Lucía's son. Now she knows who the woman is.

She is tired. Her face is serious. Ana is not afraid. The woman looks at Ana. Ana looks at the woman.

The woman goes to the counter and puts the letter next to Tomás's letter. She leaves it in
Tomás's place. Neither of them speaks.

Ana looks at Lucía's face. Then she looks at the name on the letter. She does not ask who it is.

The woman leaves her hand on the counter for a moment. She looks at the two letters. Then she walks toward the
door.

Ana can take the new letter. She can ask a question. She does neither of the two things. The woman
goes out through the door. Ana does not call her. Ana does not get up.

The boat's light is still moving on the water. Ana sees it through the window. Then she looks back
at the letters. Tomás Ferrer's name is on top of one. The woman is far away, and she is walking toward the beach.

Ana goes back to her chair. The chair faces the counter. Ana does not take either letter. She leaves them there, in
Tomás's place.

The door is open. The sea is making noise. The boat's light is moving far away. Ana
is watching the counter, and she says nothing.
