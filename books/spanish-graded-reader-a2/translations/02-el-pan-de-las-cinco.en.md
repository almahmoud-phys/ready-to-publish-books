# Story 2 — The Five O'Clock Bread

The bakery opens at five in the morning. It is the first open door in Puerto Lento.

Ana closes the kiosk at one, sleeps little, and gets to the bakery at five. She has the letter
in her pocket.

Rosa Delgado works here. Rosa is fifty-eight years old and has worked in the bakery
forever. She knows everyone in town. She knows the parents of everyone in town.

"Good morning, Ana."

"Good morning, Rosa."

There are two or three people in the bakery. Port people, people who work early. Nobody
talks much at this hour. They all drink coffee and watch the door.

Ana orders bread and coffee. Then she looks at Rosa.

"Rosa, can I ask you something?"

"One thing or many things?" says Rosa.

"One."

"Only one?" says Rosa, and she smiles. "Since when do you ask for only one thing?"

Ana smiles too. Rosa always does this. Rosa answers with questions.

"Who is Tomás Ferrer?"

Rosa does not answer. She cuts the bread.

"Why are you asking that?" says Rosa.

"Because I want to know."

"And why do you want to know now? After three months here?"

"Rosa, do you know him or not?"

Rosa puts the bread on the table. She looks at Ana.

"Who's talking about him? Beto?"

"Nobody is talking about him. That's the problem."

Ana takes the letter out of her pocket and puts it on the table, with the name facing up.

**Tomás Ferrer**

Rosa looks at the name. She does not touch the letter. She says nothing.

Ana waits. The bakery is warm and smells of bread. Outside it is still dark.

Rosa speaks slowly. She asks a question, but it is not the question Ana expects.

"Where is it?"

Ana does not understand.

"What?"

"The letter," says Rosa. "Where is it?"

Ana opens her mouth to answer. Then she closes it.

Rosa does not ask who Tomás Ferrer is. She does not ask why Ana has a letter with that name.
She does not ask what the letter says.

She asks where the letter is.

So Rosa knows who Tomás Ferrer is. She knows perfectly well. And the only thing she wants to know
is the place.

"In the street," says Ana. "Near the pier."

It is a lie. Ana does not know why she tells this lie. But she tells it.

Rosa says nothing for a long time. She cuts the bread, puts the bread in the bag, wipes the table.
She does everything slowly.

Ana waits. She knows that if she speaks now, Rosa will not answer.

Rosa looks at her for a long time.

"In the street," says Rosa.

"Yes."

"All right."

Rosa picks up Ana's bread and puts more bread in the bag. Much more bread.

"Rosa, it's too much. I can't pay for all this."

"Today you don't pay," says Rosa. "Today the bread is on me."

"Why?"

Rosa goes into the kitchen and does not answer.

Ana leaves the bakery with the bag of bread and the letter in her pocket. Outside the day is beginning.
The sky is grey and then white.

In the street, Ana opens the bag and looks at the bread. It is a lot of bread. It is too much bread for one person.

Then Ana understands something she does not want to understand.

She asks about a name. Rosa does not answer. Rosa gives her bread.

In Puerto Lento, when a person asks something they should not ask, people do not say "no."
People give bread, or say "there's nothing," or look at the sea.

Ana gets home. She puts the bag on the table. She is hungry and very tired.

But she does not eat the bread.

The bread sits on the table all morning. Ana looks at it from the bed and does not touch it.
