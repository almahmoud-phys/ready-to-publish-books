# Story 1 — The Letter with No Owner

Ana works at night. She opens the kiosk at seven and closes it at one.

The kiosk is on the pier, facing the sea. It is small: one door, one window, coffee, bread and
water. From the window, Ana watches the water all night long.

Ana has worked here since March. Ana is not the first person in this kiosk. There is another person
before her. Ana knows nothing about that person: she does not know their name and she does not know where they are now. In
Puerto Lento nobody talks about that.

At that hour there is almost nobody. Ana is alone. She is fine alone. The sea makes noise and she
listens to the noise. It is quiet work.

Tonight, when Ana comes back from the bathroom, there is a letter on the counter.

Ana looks at the letter. It is white and small. It is not old. Someone leaves it here, tonight, now.

Ana looks out the window. There is nobody outside. The street is empty.

Ana picks up the letter. On the letter there is a name:

**Tomás Ferrer**

Ana does not know that name. In Puerto Lento everyone knows everyone, but she does not know this name.

There is no address. Only the name.

At twelve her brother Beto arrives. Beto works in the boat shed. He always arrives at
twelve, always tired, always wanting coffee.

"Good evening," says Beto.

"Good evening," says Ana. "Beto, who is Tomás Ferrer?"

Beto does not answer. He drinks his coffee. He looks at the sea.

"Beto?"

"There's nothing," says Beto.

"What do you mean there's nothing? I have a letter. It's here, in my hand. With a name."

Beto looks at the letter. He looks at Ana. Then he looks at the sea again.

"There's nothing, Ana."

Beto finishes the coffee and leaves. He does not say goodbye.

Ana is alone again. She has the letter in her hand.

She thinks: it's easy. The letter isn't mine. The name doesn't belong to anyone here. I can put it in the trash.

But she does not put it in the trash. She puts it in her pocket.

At one, Ana closes up and walks home along the pier. It is a clear night and the sea is
calm.

Then she looks at the water.

Far away, very far away, there is a light.

It is not a boat. A boat makes noise, and this light makes no noise. It is small and yellow. It does not come
to town and it does not go away. It is simply there.

Ana watches the light for a long time. Ten minutes. Then more.

Ana touches the letter in her pocket.

She has lived here for three months. Three months of quiet nights, of a town where nothing happens.
Ana is fine that way. She does not ask and nobody asks.

But tonight she has a letter with a name, and there is a light on the water, and her brother says there
is nothing.

Ana walks home.

She does not put the letter in the trash and she does not leave it on the table. She puts it under the bed, with the
important things.

It is a small thing. But Ana knows what it is.

The letter arrives with another person's name.

Now it is hers.
